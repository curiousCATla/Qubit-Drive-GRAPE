\section{Motivation}
\label{sec:motivation}

The cost function that trains every logical-gate pulse in this project is not just a fidelity.
It is a fidelity plus four penalty terms:
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cost}
\mathcal{C}(u) \;=\; -\,\overline{F_{\mathrm{coh}}}(u)
\;+\; \lamdisc \sum_{i \neq j} \bigl(F_i - F_j\bigr)^2
\;+\; \lamd\, g_{d}(u)
\;+\; \lamb\, g_{b}(u)
\;+\; \lama\, g_{a}(u),
\end{equation}
%
where $\overline{F_{\mathrm{coh}}}$ is the coherent process fidelity averaged over the training
truncations (Heeres et al.\ Supp.\ Eq.~23), the $\lamdisc$ term is the cross-truncation
discrepancy penalty (Supp.\ Eq.~24), and $g_d, g_b, g_a$ are the derivative, boundary, and soft
amplitude penalties defined in \texttt{core/grape\_core.py}.

Every penalty term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:cost} is non-negative and can only \emph{raise} the cost.
That has an uncomfortable consequence: if the four weights are tuned to maximize the training
objective, the tuning will drive all four to zero. The optimizer will happily oblige, and it will
return a pulse that is rougher, wider in bandwidth, and better at exploiting the finite
Hilbert-space truncation it was trained on. The training number goes up; the pulse gets worse.

So the weights cannot be chosen by optimizing the same quantity the weights appear in. They have
to be chosen against something training never sees. This report does that in three steps: it
sweeps the weights, scores each candidate on the \emph{reported} gate fidelity over
\emph{held-out} cavity truncations, and then picks a recipe subject to a hardware spectral-width
budget.

\section{What is measured}
\label{sec:metrics}

The distinction that matters throughout is between the \textbf{training objective} and the
\textbf{reported metric}.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $F_{\mathrm{coh}}$ --- the coherent process fidelity the optimizer actually descends on,
        evaluated at the training truncations $n_c \in \{22, 24, 26\}$. This is what
        \texttt{optimize\_multi\_state\_pulse} returns.
  \item $F_{\mathrm{held}}$ --- the Pedersen gate fidelity, computed from the \emph{full} $D
        \times D$ propagator, projected onto the logical subspace
        ($\texttt{logical\_block} = B^{\dagger} U B$), and evaluated at cavity truncations
        \emph{not} used in training. This is the headline number, produced by
        \texttt{main.report\_pedersen\_gate\_fidelity}.
\end{itemize}

Alongside the fidelity, each candidate carries pulse-cost and robustness metrics: the
\textbf{99\% occupied spectral width} $W_{99}$ of each drive, expressed as a share of that
drive's own band mask; the peak drive amplitude; a per-step roughness (RMS first difference); the
spread $\max - \min$ of $F_{\mathrm{held}}$ across the held-out set; and the mean leakage $L_1$
out of the logical subspace. The scored cost is
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:frac}
\texttt{occ99\_frac} \;=\; \max\!\left(\frac{W_{99}^{C}}{\SI{54}{\mega\hertz}},\;
\frac{W_{99}^{T}}{\SI{66}{\mega\hertz}}\right),
\end{equation}
%
the share of its own allowance consumed by the worse drive, and the budget is a percentage of it.
Section~\ref{sec:width} derives why $W_{99}$, and not the RMS spectral width the sweep originally
scored, is the defensible statistic, and why the two drives are normalised separately.

\subsection{The spectral cost statistic}
\label{sec:width}

For a complex drive $\epsilon$ with normalized power spectrum
$p(f) = |\hat\epsilon(f)|^2 / \sum_{f'} |\hat\epsilon(f')|^2$, define
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:w99}
W_{99} \;=\; \min_{[a,b]}\,(b-a)
\qquad \text{subject to} \qquad
\sum_{a \leq f \leq b} p(f) \;\geq\; 0.99 .
\end{equation}
%
This is the narrowest window carrying 99\% of the power. It is computed by
\texttt{analysis.penalty\_sweep.occupied\_width} in $O(N \log N)$ (sort the \texttt{fftfreq} axis,
cumulative-sum, one \texttt{searchsorted} per left edge), and its value is quantised to the FFT
bin spacing $1/(N\,\mathrm{d}t) = \SI{0.909}{\mega\hertz}$.

\paragraph{The metric it replaces was partly a gauge artifact.}
The sweep originally scored \texttt{bandwidth\_MHz}, the second \emph{raw} moment of the power
spectrum --- the RMS frequency about $f = 0$. By the K\"onig--Huygens identity this decomposes
exactly as
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kh}
\langle f^2 \rangle \;=\; \langle f \rangle^2 \;+\; \sigma_f^2 ,
\end{equation}
%
so a quantity reported as a ``width'' in fact carries the spectral \emph{centroid} inside it. The
centroid is not a property of the waveform. The lab-frame field is
$\mathrm{Re}\bigl[\epsilon(t)\,e^{i\omega_{\mathrm{LO}} t}\bigr]$, and the pair
$(\epsilon, \omega_{\mathrm{LO}})$ is redundant: the substitution
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gauge}
\epsilon(t) \;\longmapsto\; \epsilon(t)\,e^{-2\pi i f_0 t},
\qquad
\omega_{\mathrm{LO}} \;\longmapsto\; \omega_{\mathrm{LO}} + 2\pi f_0
\end{equation}
%
leaves the physical field identical while sending $\langle f\rangle \to \langle f\rangle - f_0$.
Under Eq.~\eqref{eq:gauge}, $\sigma_f$ and $W_{99}$ are invariant and $\mathrm{BW}_{\mathrm{rms}}$
is not. Measured on the two pulses at the centre of this study, $\langle f\rangle^2$ supplies
\textbf{26\%} of $\langle f^2\rangle$ for the incumbent recipe and \textbf{51\%} for the
$\lamd = 0$ control. Retuning the transmon drive LO by \SI{12}{\mega\hertz} --- routine, and free
--- drops the control's reported bandwidth from \num{16.90} to \SI{15.59}{\mega\hertz}, and
centring it exactly would give \SI{11.85}{\mega\hertz}, with no change whatever to the waveform.

\paragraph{It also double-counted a constraint already enforced exactly.}
The gauge-independent content of the RMS figure --- \emph{which} lab-frame frequencies carry power
--- is the spectral support, and that is already pinned by the band-limit projection of
\texttt{core/fourier\_cutoff.py} as a hard orthogonal projection: the measured power outside the
mask is $\sim\!\num{6.5e-32}$ of the total. Charging for support a second time, softly, only
creates something the optimizer can trade away.

\paragraph{Why not simply the centroid-referred spread $\sigma_f$.}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:kh} suggests dropping the $\langle f\rangle^2$ term and scoring $\sigma_f$, which is
gauge-invariant and fixes the first defect. It does not fix the second: any $f^2$-weighted
statistic is tail-dominated, and here the tails are exactly where the mask truncates. The fraction
of the $f^2$ weight contributed by $|f| > \SI{20}{\mega\hertz}$ is \textbf{30.7\%} for the
incumbent and \textbf{68.5\%} for the control, so the statistic is most sensitive precisely where
it is least meaningful. $W_{99}$ weights each bin by its own power and nothing more.

A ``99\% width symmetric about the centroid'' is likewise not usable. These spectra are strongly
skewed (skewness $-1.05$ and $+0.70$), and a symmetric window measures \SI{82.29}{\mega\hertz} for
the control's transmon drive --- wider than the \SI{66}{\mega\hertz} mask itself, because a window
centred at $\langle f\rangle = -12.05$ spans $[-53, +29]$ and half of it is empty spectrum. The
minimum-width construction of Eq.~\eqref{eq:w99} needs no centre at all.

\paragraph{Two drives, two allowances.}
The cavity and transmon drives do not share a spectral allowance: the hard masks are
$\pm\SI{27}{\mega\hertz}$ and $\pm\SI{33}{\mega\hertz}$, so \SI{54}{\mega\hertz} against
\SI{66}{\mega\hertz}. They are also separate hardware chains --- own LO, mixer, DAC channel and
line --- so nothing in the apparatus experiences the maximum of the two widths. Scoring
$\max(W_{99}^{C}, W_{99}^{T}) \leq \tau$ is therefore a conjunction of two constraints forced to
share one threshold, and comparing the raw widths is comparing ``20 GB used'' on a 50 GB disk
against ``25 GB used'' on a 100 GB disk.
Normalising each width by its own allowance first, as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:frac}, restores the correct
conjunction:
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:conj}
\max\!\left(\frac{x}{a},\, \frac{y}{b}\right) \leq t
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
(x \leq ta) \;\wedge\; (y \leq tb).
\end{equation}
%
This is not a distinction without a difference. Two grid configurations bind on the
\emph{cavity}, and one of them is decisive: $\lamd = 0$, $\lamb = 6\times10^{-6}$ has its cavity
drive at \textbf{87.5\%} of the cavity mask while its transmon drive sits at \textbf{66.1\%} of
the transmon mask. A transmon-only rule at 70\%, or any rule comparing raw widths without asking
what each drive was allowed, would have \emph{admitted} it --- and it is a $\lamd = 0$ negative
control with the third-highest fidelity in the study ($F_{\mathrm{held}} = \num{0.999578}$), so
admitting it would have handed it the recommendation. The per-drive rule rejects it for the
correct reason: its cavity drive consumes 87.5\% of the cavity's own allowance.

\paragraph{The budget is a percentage, not a bare number of MHz.}
$W_{99}$ is bounded above by each drive's mask width by construction, so
%
\begin{equation}
\texttt{occ99\_frac} \;\leq\; \texttt{BUDGET\_FRAC} = 0.70
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\SI{37.8}{\mega\hertz}\ \text{cavity},\quad \SI{46.2}{\mega\hertz}\ \text{transmon},
\end{equation}
%
a self-documenting and dimensionless threshold. This form is deliberate. The candidate statistics
live on different scales over this sweep (\texttt{bandwidth\_MHz} spans \numrange{5.02}{18.31},
$\sigma_f$ \numrange{4.23}{14.30}, $W_{99}$ \numrange{17.27}{64.55} in MHz), so the old
\SI{16}{\mega\hertz} threshold carried across to $\sigma_f$ would admit \emph{every}
configuration and hand the recommendation to a $\lamd = 0$ negative control --- the exact
configuration this study exists to reject. A percentage cannot be mis-ported that way.
\texttt{validation/test\_pulse\_metrics.py} pins the gauge invariance, the conjunction
equivalence of Eq.~\eqref{eq:conj}, that selection failure, and the fact that the binding drive is
not always the transmon.

\paragraph{The recommendation does not change.}
Calibrated to equal strictness --- 28 of the 33 distinct configurations feasible --- all four
statistics select the same recipe, $\lamd = 10^{-6}$ with the other weights at incumbent, at
$F_{\mathrm{held}} = \num{0.999236}$ (seed-averaged over $n = 3$):
\texttt{bandwidth\_MHz} $\leq 16.0$, $\sigma_f \leq 10.0$,
$W_{99} \leq \SI{44.9}{\mega\hertz}$, and \texttt{occ99\_frac} $\leq 0.70$ all agree. Their rank
correlations over the sweep are \num{0.99} (RMS--$\sigma_f$), \num{0.95} (RMS--$W_{99}$) and
\num{0.96} ($\sigma_f$--$W_{99}$). This is a metric-correctness fix, not a result-changing one,
and the same holds for the per-drive normalisation: at 70\% of each drive's own mask, 14 of the 15
OFAT and 20 of the 25 grid configurations remain feasible and the two sweeps select
$\lamd = 10^{-6}$ ($F = \num{0.999236}$) and $\lamd = 3\times10^{-6}$, $\lamb = 6\times10^{-5}$
($F = \num{0.997947}$) exactly as before. Selection is also stable under replication: the same
recipe is chosen seed-by-seed, even though Section~\ref{sec:replication} shows its margin over
the incumbent is not separated from noise at $n = 3$. What changes is the \emph{margin} and the
\emph{reason}: the nearest rejected configuration now misses by 9.1 percentage points of its own
allowance rather than \SI{0.9}{\mega\hertz}, and one rejection is now attributed to the drive that
actually causes it.

\subsection{Experimental design}

Only the four $\lambda$'s vary. Everything else --- the smooth random warm start, the training
truncations $[22,24,26]$, the hard frequency bands ($\pm\SI{27}{\mega\hertz}$ cavity,
$\pm\SI{33}{\mega\hertz}$ transmon), the amplitude limits (\num{40} rad/$\mu$s), $N = 550$, and
$\mathrm{d}t = \SI{2}{\nano\second}$ --- is pinned in \texttt{analysis.penalty\_sweep.FIXED}.
Two search modes are used: a one-factor-at-a-time (OFAT) ladder over each axis in turn with the
others at their incumbent values, and a focused two-dimensional grid over $\lamd \times \lamb$.
The OFAT axes are $\lamd$, $\lamb$, and the amplitude \emph{threshold} $\epsilon_{\max}$; the
reason $\epsilon_{\max}$ appears in place of the weight $\lama$ is given in
Section~\ref{sec:inert}. A $\lamdisc$ ladder was run and then retired for the same reason, and
its five rows are retained --- as the noise measurement of Section~\ref{sec:floors} rather than
as an axis.

Because L-BFGS-B on this landscape is non-convex, the two axes that carry claims ($\lamd$ and
$\lamb$) are trained from three random seeds and the figures carry the across-seed standard
deviation. A fidelity gap smaller than that spread is not a ranking. The seed replication is
what the retired $\lamdisc$ ladder and the trimmed grid paid for: Section~\ref{sec:floors} shows
that at a single seed, neither $\lamb$ nor the recommendation's margin can be separated from a
reseed, so replication --- not more grid cells --- is where the compute belongs.

The $\lambda = 0$ rows are the control that makes the whole exercise meaningful. If removing a

\subsection{Two of the four weights are inert}
\label{sec:inert}

The sweep's first substantive result is negative, and it is worth stating before anything else
because it changes what the remaining figures can possibly show. Two of the four weights in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:cost} have no effect under these conditions. Both were confirmed at convergence
against the canonical production pulse \texttt{pulses/u\_X\_main.npy}, not merely in an
under-trained regime.

\paragraph{$\lama$ is exactly inert.}
The soft amplitude penalty is $g_a(u) = \sum \max\bigl(0, |u| - \epsilon_{\max}\bigr)^2$ with
$\epsilon_{\max} = \SI{40}{\radian\per\micro\second}$. Converged pulses here peak near
\SI{17}{\radian\per\micro\second}, so \texttt{amplitude\_penalty} returns exactly \num{0.0} with
an exactly zero gradient. Multiplying zero by any weight changes nothing: the measured range in
$F_{\mathrm{coh}}$ across $\lama \in [0, 8\times10^{-4}]$ is \num{0.0e+00}, an exact zero rather
than a small number. The live knob is the \emph{threshold}, not the weight, so this study sweeps
$\epsilon_{\max}$ over \SIrange{12}{40}{\radian\per\micro\second} --- a range that brackets the
observed peak --- in place of the dead $\lama$ ladder.

\paragraph{$\lamdisc$ is negligible.}
At convergence the three training truncations already agree to $\sim 10^{-4}$
($F_{\mathrm{coh}} = 0.997287,\, 0.997404,\, 0.997410$ at $n_c = 22, 24, 26$), so the
discrepancy cost is $\sum_{i \neq j}(F_i - F_j)^2 = \num{5.8e-8}$. Even at weight $5.0$ that
contributes \num{2.9e-7} against an $O(1)$ fidelity term --- seven orders of magnitude down.
The gradient share is no larger: $\sum_{i\neq j} 2(F_i - F_j)(g_i - g_j)$ with
$|F_i - F_j| \sim 10^{-4}$ is some four orders below the fidelity gradient it is added to. The
measured response of the \emph{training} objective across $\lamdisc \in [0,5]$ is
correspondingly $\sim 10^{-11}$.

The \emph{held-out} response is not $10^{-11}$; it is \num{1.03e-3}, and the gap between those
two numbers is the most useful thing this ladder produced. A perturbation four orders below the
gradient cannot systematically improve a pulse, but it is finite, so it does move a non-convex,
non-converged L-BFGS-B trajectory into a different local basin --- and different basins differ
in held-out fidelity at the $10^{-3}$ level. That makes the $\lamdisc$ ladder a calibrated null
experiment rather than a wasted one; Section~\ref{sec:floors} uses it as such.

It is worth being precise about why widening the \emph{scoring} range does not rescue $\lamdisc$.
The held-out truncations are a scoring set; the discrepancy term is computed \emph{inside
training}, over \texttt{trunc\_list} $= [22, 24, 26]$ only. Changing what a pulse is graded on
cannot change the gradient it was trained with. Making $\lamdisc$ bite would require more widely
separated \emph{training} truncations, which would alter \texttt{FIXED} and break comparability
with every pulse already in \texttt{pulses/}.

Neither result is a bug: both penalty terms are correctly implemented and both would activate
under different settings. The conclusion is narrower and more useful --- as the cost function is
currently configured, tuning either weight is wasted effort, and the $\lama$ term could be
removed from Eq.~\eqref{eq:cost} with no effect at all.

\subsection{Two noise floors, and which one applies}
\label{sec:floors}

Every effect size below has to be read against a floor, and this study admits two. They differ by
almost an order of magnitude, so which one is quoted decides whether a result is reported as
decisive or as marginal.

\begin{description}
  \item[$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bitwise}} = \num{1.5e-4}$.] Two configurations in this sweep are the
    same recipe to one part in $10^{16}$ --- an OFAT rung computed as
    $\texttt{BASELINE}\times\texttt{multiplier}$ landing on a value the grid typed as a literal.
    The harness hashes the exact float, so both were trained. Their scores differ by
    \num{1.5e-4}. This is reduction-order nondeterminism and nothing else: at $10^{-16}$ the two
    trajectories are the same trajectory.
  \item[$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{basin}} = \num{1.03e-3}$.] The retired $\lamdisc$ ladder spans
    $\lamdisc \in \{0, 0.05, 0.15, 0.5, 1.5, 5.0\}$ --- a hundredfold range \emph{including
    exactly zero} --- under a perturbation shown above to be dynamically null. Its held-out
    fidelities nonetheless spread by \num{1.03e-3}, non-monotonically and with no trend. This is
    the optimizer changing basins, and it is what a \emph{finite} change to any weight does.
\end{description}

$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{basin}}$ is the operative floor, because every comparison in this report is
between two configurations whose weights differ finitely. Read against it:

$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{basin}}$ was estimated before seeds were replicated, and replication has
since checked it directly: the mean across-seed standard deviation over the fifteen OFAT
configurations at $n = 3$ is \num{1.12e-3}, against the null ladder's \num{4.0e-4}. The null
experiment was a good proxy for the reseed spread, and a conservative one.

\subsection{What survives replication}
\label{sec:replication}

Because every configuration shares the same three seeds, the informative contrast is
\emph{paired}: differencing two configurations seed-by-seed cancels the basin term they hold in
common, which a comparison of two means with their own error bars does not.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
Contrast & Per-seed differences & Mean & Paired $t(2)$ \\
\midrule
$\lamd = 10^{-4}$ vs.\ incumbent (control) & $-9.0, -7.2, -9.5$ & \num{-8.56e-3} & $-12.0$ \\
Recommendation vs.\ incumbent              & $+2.5, +1.2, +0.8$ & \num{+1.48e-3} & $+2.9$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\\[2pt]
{\footnotesize Differences in units of $10^{-3}$. The two-tailed 5\% critical value at 2 dof is
$4.30$.}
\end{center}

\paragraph{$\lamd$ is real.} Monotone over the full ladder, spread \num{1.04e-2}, and the
positive control clears the bar by a factor of three. The method detects an effect of that size
comfortably at $n = 3$.

\paragraph{$\lamb$ is unidentified.} The range of its seed-means is \num{6.4e-4} --- smaller
than the \num{1.29e-3} standard deviation of any \emph{single point} on the axis. At $n = 1$ it
presented as a \num{2.2e-3} effect; replication shows that was basin noise. It is reported here
as unidentified rather than weak, and the same applies to $\epsilon_{\max}$.

\paragraph{The recommendation is directionally consistent but not established.} It wins on all
three seeds, but at $t(2) = 2.9$ it does not clear the 5\% bar. The margin also \emph{shrank}
under replication, from the \num{2.47e-3} measured at seed 42 alone to \num{1.48e-3}: seed 42 was
the most favourable of the three, which is precisely the failure mode a single-seed study is
exposed to. The defensible claim is that $\lamd$ should be reduced from its incumbent value and
that the trend across the ladder supports it --- not that $10^{-6}$ specifically beats
$10^{-5}$. Separating those two needs more seeds, not more axes: an effect $5.8\times$ larger
cleared the bar at $n = 3$, so this one needs of order a decade more samples, or a design that
pairs on something beyond the seed.

This is the finding that redirected the compute budget from grid cells to seed replication, and
it is the reason the trimmed design is not merely cheaper but more informative than the one it
replaces.

The grid uses the two axes that plausibly move the pulse. $\lamd$ controls waveform smoothness
and therefore bandwidth; $\lamb$ controls how hard the drive is pinned to zero at the pulse
edges, which constrains the same waveform through a different mechanism, so an interaction
between them is plausible. A $\lamd \times \lamdisc$ grid was the original design, but by
Section~\ref{sec:inert} it would be constant along the $\lamdisc$ axis.

The grid shown is the full $5\times5$ as run. Its durable finding is that the response is
effectively \emph{one}-dimensional: variation along $\lamd$ is \numrange{6.3e-3}{1.21e-2} at
every fixed $\lamb$, while variation along $\lamb$ is \numrange{8.1e-4}{4.81e-3} --- straddling
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{basin}}$ and non-monotone in all five rows. Having established that, the
harness's grid is trimmed to the low-$\lamd$ $3\times3$ corner that brackets the recommended
$\lamd = 10^{-6}$; all nine of those cells are already trained, so the retained interaction check
now costs nothing and the $5\times5$ need not be re-run.

The selection rule is deliberately simple and stated in advance, so it cannot be retrofitted to
whatever the data happened to show: \emph{among configurations for which every drive occupies at
most \texttt{BUDGET\_FRAC} of its own band mask, take the highest mean held-out Pedersen fidelity;
break ties toward lower $W_{99}$.} By Eq.~\eqref{eq:conj} that per-drive conjunction is exactly the
single filter \texttt{occ99\_frac} $\leq$ \texttt{BUDGET\_FRAC}. The
budget is a property of the control hardware, not of the optimization, which is why it enters as a
constraint rather than as another term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:cost}. Five of the 38 configurations are
rejected by it, and all five have $\lamd = 0$: the constraint excludes exactly one recipe family
rather than a scattering of outliers.

\section{Caveats}
\label{sec:caveats}

\paragraph{The held-out set includes under-resolved truncations.}
The scoring range extends down to $n_c = 16$, which does not fully contain the
$\alpha = \sqrt{3}$ cat ($\bar{n} = 3$). Those low truncations depress the held-out mean and
dominate the robustness spread for \emph{every} recipe, including good ones. They are kept
because a pulse that exploits the truncation wall fails there first --- that is exactly the
failure this sweep exists to detect --- but the absolute value of $F_{\mathrm{held}}$ should not
be quoted as ``the gate fidelity.'' The per-truncation breakdown in the manifest JSON is the
honest view.

\paragraph{The scored width \emph{is} an occupied-bandwidth figure.}
An earlier version of this report warned that the scored statistic was an RMS spectral width,
useful for ranking but ``not an occupied-bandwidth or $-\SI{3}{\decibel}$ figure.'' That caveat
is now discharged in the reader's favour: the scored statistic is $W_{99}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:w99},
which is exactly an occupied-bandwidth figure at a 99\% power criterion; it is invariant under the
drive-LO gauge freedom of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gauge}; and it is charged per drive against that drive's
own allowance. It remains a \emph{soft} budget applied after training. The hard and the soft limit
are both per-drive and they are not the same limit: the band-limit projection
(\texttt{core/fourier\_cutoff.py}) fixes where power may exist at all, is applied to the controls
and the gradient at every iteration, and is never violated regardless of the penalty weights;
$W_{99}$ measures how much of that allowance a converged pulse actually consumes, which the
projection cannot express.

\paragraph{Seed variance is part of the result.}
The optimizer is non-convex and warm-started randomly. Differences in held-out fidelity smaller
than the across-seed standard deviation reported in Table~\ref{tab:summary} do not establish that
one recipe beats another, and by Section~\ref{sec:floors} that threshold is
$\sim\!\num{1e-3}$, not $\sim\!\num{1e-4}$. Seeds are replicated across the OFAT ladders; the
grid remains single-seed, so its cells carry no error bar and the one-dimensionality it
establishes rests on the consistency of the $\lamd$ ordering across its five rows rather than on
any individual cell.

\paragraph{The recommendation is per-gate.}
The sweep is run for one logical gate at a time. A recipe selected on $X$ is a reasonable
starting point for the other gates, not a validated setting for them.

\section{Reproducing}
\label{sec:repro}

\begin{lstlisting}
# OFAT ladder, 3 seeds, as 3 concurrent shards. One config occupies only
# ~2.4 cores, so a serial sweep leaves most of a 10-core machine idle.
for i in 0 1 2; do
  python analysis/penalty_sweep.py --gate X --mode ofat \
         --seeds 42 43 44 --maxiter 1500 --shard $i/3 &
done; wait
python analysis/penalty_sweep.py --gate X --mode ofat \
       --seeds 42 43 44 --maxiter 1500 --merge

# Focused 2-D grid (all cells cached; completes in seconds)
python analysis/penalty_sweep.py --gate X --mode grid \
       --grid-x deriv --grid-y boundary --seeds 42 43 44 --maxiter 1500

# Figures, budgeted recommendation, and tables
#   -> set BUDGET_FRAC (% of each drive's own mask) in
#      penalty_optimization.ipynb, run with RUN_SWEEP = False

# Metric + selection acceptance tests
python validation/test_pulse_metrics.py -v
\end{lstlisting}

Both trained pulses and scored metric rows are disk-cached under
\texttt{results/penalty\_sweep\_cache/}, keyed by a hash of the full configuration, so an
interrupted sweep resumes without retraining and re-scoring never re-optimizes. Shards share
that cache and write disjoint rows, so they need no locking; \texttt{-{}-merge} rebuilds the
combined table from it in the order a serial run would have produced, and refuses to emit a
partial table if any row is missing.

Each trained configuration also keeps the pulse a run truncated at 500 and at 1000 iterations
would have returned (\texttt{SNAPSHOT\_ITERS}), scored into \texttt{F\_ped\_heldout\_mean@500}
and \texttt{@1000}. The optimizer-side cost is a callback that copies an array and the scoring
cost is $\sim\!1.7\%$ of training, in exchange for a maxiter-sensitivity curve on every
configuration.

The question it exists to answer is whether a short screening stage preserves the \emph{ranking}
of configurations. On the 30 configurations that carry snapshots, the Spearman correlation
between the 500-iteration ordering and the final one is \num{0.88} and \num{0.94} on the two
seeds; the top-3 sets agree exactly on both, and the eventual winner is in the 500-iteration
top-3 every time. A screen at one third of the iteration budget is therefore a sound shortlisting
tool.

It is worth being clear about how much that buys, though. With only fifteen configurations per
gate, screening then retraining the survivors saves around a third of the cost of extending to
all six logical gates. The larger lever is the one Section~\ref{sec:replication} supplies: since
$\lamb$ and $\epsilon_{\max}$ are unidentified, a per-gate sweep needs only the six-rung $\lamd$
ladder, which is a $2.5\times$ reduction before any screening is applied. Replication paid for
itself twice --- once in what it established, and once in what it allowed to be dropped.
